## The Shift

**Intelligence is commoditizing; the durable value is business alpha.**

For two years the market assumed the AI era's value would concentrate in the intelligence layer —
the frontier models. The evidence — and the history of every platform wave — says the models are
becoming a metered commodity sold by a rich oligopoly, while the value that endures is what a
company owns and the model layer can't take: its proprietary data, workflows, customer trust, and
know-how — **its business alpha.**

---

## Why now — what just happened

In the past five months, Anthropic shipped first-party products into five categories its own
customers and partners had proven: coding (Claude Code, overtaking Cursor — once ~40–50% of
Anthropic's revenue), design (Claude Design, launched three days after Anthropic's product chief
resigned from Figma's board), security, legal, and science — including an internal drug-discovery
program. OpenAI, Microsoft, Amazon, and Anthropic all stood up billion-dollar "forward-deployed
engineering" units in a single week of July to embed inside enterprises. Palantir's CEO went on
CNBC to say out loud what enterprise buyers say in private: customers want *"control over their
compute, their models, their data stack and their alpha."* And the price of a fixed level of AI
intelligence keeps falling roughly ten-fold per year, with open-weight models holding 3–6 months
behind the frontier.

The frontier labs don't take your bytes — **they take your business.** Their commercial terms forbid
training on your data (we verified them), and they don't need it: **they watch the traffic prove
the category, pay wholesale for the intelligence their customers buy retail, and when a market is
demonstrated they ship it themselves, at a price the customer-turned-competitor cannot match.
Legal, uninsurable — and no contract can stop it.**

## Infrastructure eating its verticals is the oldest story in tech

- **Microsoft is the defining precedent.** It owned the operating system — then took the
  application layer built on top of it, category by category: **Lotus 1-2-3 became Excel.
  WordPerfect became Word. Netscape became Internet Explorer.** The platform didn't just host the
  winners; it became them. That is the most direct analogy for what the frontier labs are doing to
  the layer built on their models today.
- **Amazon vs. its own marketplace sellers** — the platform watched everyone's sales data, then
  built the winners itself (documented by the WSJ; binding EU commitments followed).
- **Google's zero-click search** — 58% of searches ended without a click in 2024; 68% by early
  2026. The platform absorbed the answer and the layer above lost the customer.
- **The twist from Oracle and AWS**: the engine layer never went cheap — the leaders kept fat
  margins for decades *while the application layer also created massive value*. The casualties were
  second-tier platforms and the app companies that fumbled the transition.

The new part: today's platform keeps AWS-grade margins **and** runs the Amazon playbook against
its own ecosystem — at software speed.

## What this means for our investing

Every deal we see, in every group, now carries a supply-chain question none of our existing lenses
asked: **when this product runs, where does the alpha flow — does it stay home, or drain up to the
lab?** Two failure modes, one prize:

- ❌ **The company leaks its own alpha.** Its differentiating data or workflow transits a frontier
  lab's API at volume every day; its engineers use consumer AI tools that *do* train on their
  content by default; it signed a data-for-early-access partnership; it cannot switch providers.
  It is subsidizing its future competitor's market research.
- ❌ **The company is a conduit for its clients' alpha.** Its product requires its client
  companies to pipe *their* proprietary or regulated data through third-party models, in exactly
  the industries (health, defense, financial, legal, pharma) where buyers now rank data control as
  their #1 adoption obstacle — with no path to deploy inside the client's walls.
- ✅ **The prize: companies with real AI sovereignty.** They know precisely what their alpha is and
  keep it where the lab can't see it; they can switch models — or run open-weight ones — without
  breaking; they can deploy inside a hospital's, bank's, or defense prime's boundary; and they get
  *cheaper to run every year* as intelligence deflates. Building on frontier models is fine — the
  two biggest application-layer winners of the era did exactly that. Unexamined dependence is the
  kill signal, not dependence itself.

## The desired end state for start-ups and enterprises — own the stack that matters

Karp said it for the enterprise, and it applies to every startup we back: the winners have
*"control over their compute, their models, their data stack and their alpha"* — they own the
means of production of their own intelligence. Unpacked as an architecture, the sovereign end
state looks like this:

| Layer | Posture | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Alpha** — proprietary data, workflows, customer relationships, domain IP | **OWN** | Never leaves the company's (or its customer's) boundary; invisible to the model provider |
| **Harness** — evals, orchestration, domain scaffolding, integration depth | **OWN** | The layer that makes any model perform like a specialist — this, not the model, is the product |
| **Models** — frontier APIs, open-weight alternatives | **RENT — swappable** | Behind an abstraction layer; switch or self-host without breaking; ride the 10×/yr commoditization down |
| **Compute & deployment** — cloud, VPC, on-prem, edge | **CONTROL** | Deployed where the data demands it — inside the customer's walls when the vertical requires it |

**Own the top of the stack, control the bottom, rent the middle on your own terms.** A company
shaped like this gets cheaper to run every year, cannot be held up by its supplier, and sells
*into* the sovereignty wave instead of against it.

## Signals to look for — all NWA groups

| ✅ Stronger | ❌ Weaker |
|---|---|
| Sensitive data processed inside the company's or customer's boundary; only commodity context reaches the model | The core differentiator transits one lab's API, verbatim, at volume, daily |
| Founder can say exactly what the model provider sees, and what the data clause in the provider agreement says | "They say they don't train on our data" is the founder's entire answer |
| Can switch models — or run open-weight — and has actually tested it | Product breaks without one specific provider; no second integration, no benchmarks |
| Sovereignty demand is a tailwind: the product deploys on-prem/VPC or *strengthens* the customer's control of its own data | The pitch's data flywheel depends on aggregating customer data that customers are about to stop sharing |

## The upside category

The counter-movement is itself an investable theme: **sovereignty enablers** — open-weight model
harnesses and control planes, on-prem/private-cloud/edge inference, secure data pipelines that keep
enterprise data in-boundary, agent governance and audit. Palantir–NVIDIA just validated the
category at nation-state scale; the same demand is cascading down to enterprises and the
mid-market. Enablers are the picks-and-shovels of this shift — they win no matter which lab wins.
