## The Shift

**AI agents are becoming the substrate through which problems get solved — and the durability of any startup we back now depends on whether its founder is solving the problem *as agents will reshape it*, not as it exists today.**

Alongside Structural Discontinuity (*is this riding a genuine, irreversible shift?*) and Memory Lock-in (*does it get stickier with use?*), every TechGroup deal now answers a third question: **does this company's value, model, and moat survive — or strengthen — when agents become the predominant actor and consumer of digital products, work, and transactions?**

---

## Why now — what just happened

- **The internet crossed over to the machines — a year early.** In June 2026 Cloudflare reported bots passed **57.5% of all web traffic**; Matthew Prince had forecast the crossover for 2027. Agentic traffic — bots acting *on behalf of* a human — grew nearly **8,000% in one year** and runs at roughly **1,000:1**: one human intention spawns a thousand machine requests.
- **The builders say knowledge work is next.** Boris Cherny (Anthropic, creator of Claude Code) calls coding largely *solved* — 100% of his code is agent-written; he ships 10–30 PRs a day directing agent fleets. His forecast: ~100× more people *directing* work than *doing* it. Andrej Karpathy supplies the timescale — the **decade of agents**, autonomy advancing one notch at a time across every function.
- **Grassroots demand is already here.** OpenClaw — an open-source autonomous agent that lives in your messaging apps and *acts* for you — earned **100,000 GitHub stars in 48 hours** in January. The human-machine interface is flipping: you stop operating software and start delegating to an agent that operates it for you.
- **The boardroom has conceded the point.** Bret Taylor (Chair of OpenAI): *"the vast majority of the digital interactions you'll have with your customers will be via your agent."* The Economist — a 180-year-old publisher — is building a **two-track internet: one for humans, one for AI agents.** When the incumbents restructure around the question, the question is settled.

## The real test — agents reimagine problems, not interfaces

The shallow version of this idea asks *"is the product built for humans or bots?"* — an interface checklist (`llms.txt`, an API). Useful plumbing, weak thesis. **The real test operates one level deeper: does a future of capable agents *reimagine, dissolve, or transform the problem this startup solves* — and is the founder building for that world or this one?**

The doorway question on every deal:

> **"Is this information for a human to act on, or a transaction for an agent to complete?"**

A worked example: a beautiful comparison-shopping site — clean UX, great SEO, affiliate revenue — is an excellent answer to *"a human needs to compare and decide."* But in the agent era the human doesn't browse; their agent queries structured offers and transacts. The problem itself **dissolves**. Traffic becomes a thousand unmonetized bot page-loads; the affiliate funnel collapses. The founder who *sees* this builds a structured offer-and-transaction layer agents can call, priced on completed transactions. Same domain, **reimagined problem**. That founder is durable. The first one is roadkill with good design.

## Where it bites first

**Work & the firm.** The unit of production flips from human-hour to agent-task — a 10-person agent-native company delivers what a 1,000-person firm did. **Per-seat SaaS pricing is exposed at the model level**: when agents do the work, seats stop counting anything real. This is live, not slideware — Bret Taylor's Sierra already charges **only when the agent resolves the customer's problem**. Headcount-heavy operating plans start reading as liabilities; the impressive answer to "how will you scale the team?" may be *"we won't."*

**Commerce & markets.** The buyer becomes an agent: discovery, comparison, and purchase run through a program that wants structured facts and the best transaction — not ads, brand affect, or landing pages. Aggregators and attention-middle layers get routed around; value re-concentrates at the **transactional plumbing** and the **source of supply**. And the 1,000:1 load ratio is a P&L event: a business that monetizes per human view but serves per agent request has a **cost-revenue scissors** opening underneath it.

## The four postures — half the verdict is naming where a company stands

| Posture | Definition | Verdict logic |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ **Threatened** | Agents reimagine or dissolve the problem it solves. Excellent solution for a world that is ending. | Default toward pass. The good design makes it *more* dangerous — it hides the rot. |
| ✅ **Riding** | The company *is* the reimagined solution — it solves the problem as agents reshape it. | The prize. Verify it is real, not narrated. |
| ✅ **Enabling** | Picks-and-shovels for the agent economy — the rails agents run on. | Often the most underwritable bet; wins regardless of which application-layer winners emerge. |
| ✅ **Insulated** | Genuinely un-disruptable — physical, high-trust, human-verified, locally embedded. | Durable, but test the claim hard. Most "insulated" companies are Threatened and don't know it. |

The single most important discrimination this lens forces: **a company that is *Riding* the shift vs. one that is *Threatened* but tells a Riding story.** A polished "AI-powered" deck is frequently a Threatened company wearing Riding's clothes.

## What stays durable

1. **Un-scrapable value** — physical services, high-trust human relationships
2. **Flywheels that compound under agent load** — data that gets richer, not cheaper, as machine usage scales
3. **Transactional plumbing** — paid when an agent acts, not when a person looks
4. **Intelligent / physical infrastructure** — the rails, not the ride
5. **Human-verification & authenticity premiums** — proving humanness in a machine-saturated ecosystem
6. **Governance of memory** — context expiration, forgetting, control over what agents retain

And Taylor's incumbent-side sharpening: when agents can generate the software, **value migrates from production to trust** — what the customer buys is the accumulated hardening and institutional trust no competitor can mint overnight.

## Signals to look for

| ✅ Stronger | ❌ Weaker |
|---|---|
| Founder describes the problem *as agents reshape it* — a reimagined problem, not a faster interface | "AI-powered" gloss on today's workflow — makes a human doer 20% faster at a task agents will own outright |
| Outcome / usage / transaction pricing — paid when the job completes | Per-seat revenue; operating plan scales headcount linearly with revenue |
| Callable by agents and monetized on the transaction; cost model survives machine-volume traffic | Ad- or engagement-funded; margin assumes human-scale traffic; bot-blocking as the entire strategy |
| Durability rests on un-scrapable ground: transactions, physical, trust, verification, compounding data | "Insulated" claim asserted, not tested — the default state of companies that haven't looked |
