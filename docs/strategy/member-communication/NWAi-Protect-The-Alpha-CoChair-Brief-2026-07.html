Protect the Alpha

A foundational shift in where AI-era business value lives — and what it changes about how we invest
New World Angels · Investment Intelligence
Advisory to all Group Co-Chairs & Members · July 2026
The Shift: intelligence is commoditizing; the durable value is business alpha. For two years the market assumed the AI era's value would concentrate in the intelligence layer — the frontier models. The evidence — and the history of every platform wave — says the models are becoming a metered commodity sold by a rich oligopoly, while the value that endures is what a company owns and the model layer can't take: its proprietary data, workflows, customer trust, and know-how — its business alpha.
01
Why now — what just happened
  • Anthropic shipped into five of its own customers' categories since February — coding (Claude Code, overtaking Cursor, once ~40–50% of its revenue), design (launched 3 days after its product chief left Figma's board), security, legal, and science + drug discovery.
  • Four labs stood up billion-dollar embed units in one July week — Microsoft ($2.5B, 6,000 engineers), Amazon, OpenAI, Anthropic — to wire their models into enterprise workflows.
  • Enterprise buyers pushed back on camera — Palantir's CEO on CNBC: customers want "control over their compute, their models, their data stack and their alpha."
  • Intelligence keeps deflating ~10× per year at fixed capability; open-weight models hold 3–6 months behind the frontier.
02
They don't take your bytes — they take your business

The frontier labs watch your traffic prove the category, pay wholesale for the intelligence you buy retail, and when your market is demonstrated, ship it themselves — at a price you can't match. Legal, uninsurable — and no contract can stop it.

  • Consumer-tier tools DO train by default — a team pasting its codebase into consumer AI apps is leaking today, no contract violated.
  • "No-train" ≠ "no-exposure" — retention carve-outs, gated zero-retention tiers, litigation holds.
  • Opt-in data partnerships are the legal channel where domain alpha transfers outright.
03
Infrastructure eating its verticals is the oldest story in tech
  • Microsoft is the defining precedent. It owned the operating system — then took the application layer built on top of it, category by category: Lotus 1-2-3 became Excel. WordPerfect became Word. Netscape became Internet Explorer. The platform didn't just host the winners; it became them. The most direct analogy for what the labs are doing to the layer built on their models today.
  • Amazon vs. its own sellers — watched everyone's sales data, then built the winners itself (WSJ-documented; binding EU commitments followed).
  • Google zero-click — 58% of searches ended without a click in 2024; 68% by early 2026. The platform absorbed the answer.
  • The twist (Oracle, AWS): the engine layer never went cheap — leaders kept fat margins for decades while the app layer also won big. The casualties: second-tier platforms and app companies that fumbled the transition. Expect the labs to stay rich, stay dominant, and keep coming.
04
What it means for our investing
One new question on every deal: when this product runs, where does the alpha flow?
  • Leaks its own alpha — its differentiator transits one lab's API at volume daily; engineers on consumer tools; data-for-access deals; can't switch providers. It is subsidizing its future competitor's market research.
  • Conduit for its clients' alpha — requires its client companies to pipe proprietary/regulated data through third-party models in exactly the industries where data control is now the #1 buying obstacle — with no path to deploy inside the client's walls.
  • The prize: real AI sovereignty — knows its alpha and keeps it home; can switch models or run open-weight; deploys in the customer's boundary; gets cheaper to run every year. Building on frontier models is fine — the era's two biggest app-layer winners did exactly that. Unexamined dependence is the kill signal, not dependence itself.
05
The desired end state for start-ups and enterprises — own the stack that matters

Karp said it for the enterprise, and it applies to every startup we back: the winners have "control over their compute, their models, their data stack and their alpha" — they own the means of production of their own intelligence. As an architecture:

Alpha — proprietary data, workflows, customer relationships, domain IP — never leaves the company's (or its customer's) boundary; invisible to the model provider
OWN
Harness — evals, orchestration, domain scaffolding, integration depth — makes any model perform like a specialist; this, not the model, is the product
OWN
Models — frontier APIs, open-weight alternatives — behind an abstraction layer; switch or self-host without breaking; ride the 10×/yr commoditization down
RENT · SWAPPABLE
Compute & deployment — cloud, VPC, on-prem, edge — deployed where the data demands it; inside the customer's walls when the vertical requires it
CONTROL

Own the top of the stack, control the bottom, rent the middle on your own terms. A company shaped like this gets cheaper to run every year, cannot be held up by its supplier, and sells into the sovereignty wave instead of against it.

✅ Stronger❌ Weaker

Founder can say exactly what the model provider sees — and what the data clause in the provider agreement says "They say they don't train on our data" is the founder's entire answer
Can switch models — or run open-weight — and has actually tested it Product breaks without one specific provider; no second integration, no benchmarks

07
The upside category — sovereignty enablers

The counter-movement is itself an investable theme: open-weight harnesses and control planes · on-prem / private-cloud / edge inference · secure in-boundary data pipelines · agent governance and audit. Palantir–NVIDIA just validated the category at nation-state scale; the same demand is cascading to enterprises and the mid-market. These are the picks-and-shovels of the shift — they win no matter which lab wins.

"In the AI era, the intelligence is rented. The alpha is what you own. Back the founders who know exactly what their alpha is, keep it where the lab can't see it, and can change engines mid-flight — and look hard at the ones selling the locks everyone is about to need."
