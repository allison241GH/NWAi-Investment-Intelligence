"All- I'm seeing a new investor-facing screening/pitch scoring tool come out each week. At first, I was quite sure this was the path. We evaluated a couple of these, to pretty disappointing results. I'm still sure AI is part of the solution, but it's less clear to me this is actually a stand-alone business opportunity.  My guess is this is functionality that will eventually roll into Dealum, or a similar product. "

"I'm completely onboard with using the power of AI to improve our due diligence.  It's the path now and forward, without question in my mind.  That said the previous comments are right on the money.  Our partners independently are using AI extensively and submit their analysis to us, but we have been given memos that look like they are just text version re-writes of the ppt deck. They present shallow, incomplete, or flawed domain understanding. Interestingly, they also favor member conviction over cold clear analysis."

"We've thought about building and releasing an AI toolset for early stage venture investors however we've seen a number of free generic services and tools emerging in what is already a small industry and have never been convinced that there's an addressable market here. AI is going to allow bespoke analysis and becomes the customized tool of specialized analysts". 

"We have done very similar things using AI but with caveats.  To the degree that AI’s responses prompt critical thinking by our humans to continue investigating with, it’s really helpful.  But I have already seen a group or two take these kinds of AI replies and drop them into a diligence memo and call that diligence complete … dangerous."

"We've engineered a bunch of deep prompts and just distributed them all to individual investors. Letting individuals do their own analysis has been reasonably effective and represents a value of membership in our group. They burn their own tokens.". 

"I agree that a minimal set of DD guidelines, in a shared AI template, for groups who syndicate deals would be very useful. AI could play a role in syndication." 

"I don't understand how I can get big wins from a normative tool. Thiel's famous quote about finding something you know to be true but that the world believes to be false is not well accomodated by current AI."

"I was in a partner meeting today and heard "This level of analysis used to take me a week; instead, it took 2 hours". Sadly the analysis was deeply flawed for the domain discussed. I do not see application of AI without deep human domain expertise and interaction."

"Effective due diligence is not a software tools problem. Diligence in a box is a path to financial ruin. The ability to provide structure and productivity is interesting, but again, can I just pull some sample prompts off of X and get 90% of the value?"

"We invested in an AI diligence platform company. It has failed to gain traction with larger investment groups and they are pivoting to angel with mixed results and diminimous margins."

"We passed on them. The TAM for these types of products is the number of angels that actually do deep diligence beyond basic prompting, which I believe is a small minority, multiplied by the number of investible startups. We found that this serviceable market of sophisticated angel meeting investible startups was much smaller than what was presented." 

"We are working on AI guidelines, disclaimer statements, rules of AI use for members, etc. But the bottom line is that investibility requires knowledge of the member's prompting. Our sense is that we need to institutionalize the prompt structures." 

"What I'm seeing from AI is normative analysis, a path to mediocre investing. I think its value is in information aggregation productivity first." 

"We've looked at a dozen AI enabled platforms for venture investing. The investing platform companies have largely been unsatisfying as an investment themselves. An analyst with open tools can get to 80% of the value these products represent. Also they don't or cannot solve the deeper challenge of domain insight". 

"Using AI to screen only works superficially to assess stage and baseline economics. AI by its design is concensus/probability based. I have yet to have somebody explain how AI can reliably spot and elevate a discontinuous innovation to which there is no predicate." 

“AI is a productivity tool. Majority of the value is productivity in scanning existing landscapes and subject matter background. As a generic tool it’s had negative contribution to final investment decisions. We’ve implemented OpenClaw to play around with recurring market monitoring and analysis and are waiting on Grok’s agentic announcement which looks promising. We cannot use Claude for any governmental applications and have found the inference costs on Grok to be lower. ”

"We started scoring pitch decks using AI prompts. But the reality was that scores never really reflected the opportunity. The value of AI here may be that it organizes the data, call it a negotiation sheet, and provides context but it's not the scoring tool."

“We’re yet to see an agentic investor that we’re willing to invest in as a product company.  More plausible is an InvestorOS”. 

"Per your collaboration network question: we don't share deal flow or even offer syndication before diligence and term sheet locked as it actually creates sidebar activity where in several cases investors are approaching companies directly and competing with other investors. LP's competing with our microfund. Nightmare to manage. We also noticed that the incentive of network members is to not share their best opportunities and dump their trouble ones into the network. You may be able to avoid this with New World's membership structure but that's not our structure so everything stays tight until we've locked an investment."

"You heard correctly, we licensed a generalized AI tool for investment deal flow screening and analysis. It created volumes of analysis that was often wrong though that's maybe to harsh maybe more subtly fundamentally not insightful. Quantity of analysis was not quality. We turned it off not ready yet. I believe the company has recently ceased operations or has become a personal tool for the founders who were investors themselves. They may relaunch but I don't see it. Prompting seems to sufficient for productivity though I'm open to the idea that there's a next generation that might find the right capability."
