---

## What This Lens Is

The fourth always-active NWAi investing lens, alongside Structural Discontinuity,
Memory Lock-in, and Agent-Era Readiness. Where Agent-Era Readiness asks whether the
*problem* a startup solves survives the agent substrate shift, this lens asks a
supply-chain question about the startup itself:

**Who captures the alpha along its model supply chain — and can it protect its own?**

The thesis in one sentence: intelligence is commoditizing; the durable value is
business alpha. Every platform wave ends with the engine becoming a commodity sold
by a rich oligopoly while enduring value migrates to the context built on top —
and this wave adds a predator's twist: the oligopoly competes with its own
customers. What NWA underwrites is not the intelligence a company rents but the
business alpha it owns and protects.

---

## The Supplier-Predator Problem

The frontier labs are simultaneously the most valuable suppliers and the most
dangerous competitors a startup has ever had. The verified 2026 record: Anthropic
shipped first-party products into five categories its customers and partners had
proven, within five months (coding, design, security, legal, science); Claude Code
overtook Anthropic's own largest customer (Cursor) after executives privately
downplayed competitive intent; four labs stood up billion-dollar forward-deployed
engineering units in a single week to embed inside enterprises.

**The mechanism is not data theft — it is worse.** Commercial API terms at all three
major labs prohibit training on customer content, and no violation is documented.
The labs don't take the bytes — they take the business, and everything they take is
legal, uninsurable, and beyond the reach of any contract:

- They **watch traffic prove the category** — revenue concentration and usage
  patterns are demand data; no zero-data-retention agreement addresses this channel,
  because the signal is the traffic itself.
- They **pay wholesale** for the inference their customer-competitors buy retail —
  once a category is proven, they can ship it at a price the customer cannot match.
- **Consumer-tier tools train by default** (Claude Free/Pro/Max, consumer ChatGPT,
  unpaid Gemini) — a team building with consumer AI tooling leaks today, with no
  contract violated.
- **No-train ≠ no-exposure** — retention carve-outs, gated and incomplete ZDR tiers
  (unavailable entirely on some frontier models), litigation holds.
- **Opt-in data partnerships** are the legal channel where domain alpha transfers
  outright.

The diligence implication: *"they say they don't train on our data"* is the
beginning of the containment question, not the answer to it.

## What Counts as Alpha

Referenced by every read below — the company's:

- Proprietary data and data flywheel
- Workflow knowledge and domain scaffolding
- Eval sets and harness logic
- Domain IP
- Customer relationships and the usage patterns that encode them

The definition is deliberately **two-sided**: the startup's own alpha, and its
*customers'* alpha. Both failure modes walk through the same doorway.

---

## The Doorway Question

> **"When this product runs, where does the alpha flow — does it stay home, or
> drain up to the lab?"**

Walk through it and the follow-on questions are: What is the alpha? Who sees it in
transit? Can the company move house? Does its go-to-market survive customers
demanding the alpha stay home?

Member-facing framing: **"Does this company keep its alpha — and its customers' —
out of the frontier labs' hands?"**

## The Screen-Level Read: KEEPS / MIXED / LEAKS / N/A

A fast qualitative read, not a scored dimension. It routes and flags; it does not
gate, cap, or enter the Triage Conviction weights.

| Read | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **KEEPS** | The differentiating layer stays home — processed locally or pre-API, containment terms stated, portability credible — or the company sells sovereignty itself (Enabler). |
| **MIXED** | Alpha transits a frontier provider but with stated awareness: no-train terms claimed, an abstraction layer or containment roadmap described. The honest early-stage norm. |
| **LEAKS** | The differentiator or the customers' proprietary data transits a frontier provider verbatim, at volume, with no stated containment or sovereign path — or the founder's entire answer is "they don't train on our data." |
| **N/A** | No model supply chain (product calls no third-party models). Most pure Track B hardware. |

### Trip-Wire Triggers (either fires a Yellow Flag ⚠️)

1. **Customer-Alpha Conduit** — the core value prop requires customers to pipe
   regulated or proprietary data through third-party frontier APIs via the startup,
   and no sovereign deployment path (VPC / on-prem / edge) is stated. Especially
   acute in health, defense, financial, legal, and pharma — the verticals where the
   alpha is richest and sovereignty demand is hardening (53% of enterprises rank
   data control their #1 agent-adoption obstacle; the EU AI Act's high-risk tranche
   binds from August 2026).
2. **Own-Alpha Exposure** — the claimed differentiator visibly transits a single
   frontier provider with no stated containment. If the model provider examined
   this company's prompts, context, tool schemas, and traffic shape, could it
   reconstruct the product's edge or read the category's momentum?

### Captured Pattern (fires a Red Flag ❌)

Single-provider dependency **+** that lab has announced or shipped a competing
native product **+** the company's alpha transits that provider. The company is
structurally feeding its own predator. The Cursor escape (owned customers + owned
harness + sprint to its own model within a year) is the bar a rebuttal must clear.

### Latent Enabler (routing note, not a bonus)

Sovereignty-enabling positioning — open-weight harnesses and control planes,
on-prem/VPC/edge inference, data-boundary last-mile, agent governance and audit —
is the picks-and-shovels of the countermovement, validated at nation-state scale
(Palantir–NVIDIA sovereign AI stack). Note as "Latent Enabler — route to Scout for
scoring." Maps to TechGroup Theme 4 (Data Sovereignty, Security & AI Trust),
sometimes Theme 1. **No conviction bonus** — reward the company, not the category;
an Enabler can be a thin wrapper too.

## Calibration — The SAFE-Note Principle

Building on frontier APIs is the normal early-stage starting point — like a SAFE
note, neutral at intake and negotiated toward a stronger structure as the company
scales. The two best application-layer outcomes of the era (Cursor, Harvey) were
built on rented frontier models. Harvey *abandoned* its own proprietary model when
the frontier outran it and won anyway — its alpha was never the model; it was the
workflow, the domain trust, and the customer base.

**Trip-wires fire on structural conduit design or founder unawareness — never on
API usage per se.** Unexamined dependence is the kill signal, not dependence itself.
The test is whether the company knows what its alpha is, where it flows, and how it
moves house.

## The Four Postures (context for the read)

Internal analytic shorthand — the screener's KEEPS / MIXED / LEAKS read is the
member-facing translation.

| Posture | What it means | Screen translation |
|---|---|---|
| **Leaking** | Own alpha transits the provider unprotected, and/or the product is a conduit for customer alpha with no sovereign path. Feeding the entity most likely to subsume it. | LEAKS |
| **Hedged** | Builds on frontier APIs (normal start) with no-train terms, an abstraction layer, and a credible portability/sovereign roadmap. The negotiable norm. | MIXED (strong Hedged with verified containment → KEEPS) |
| **Sovereign** | Alpha architecturally contained; model-agnostic; deployable in the customer's boundary. Rides token deflation — gets cheaper to run every year. | KEEPS |
| **Enabler** | Sells sovereignty itself — the locks everyone is about to need. Wins whichever lab wins. | KEEPS + Latent Enabler routing note |

Most companies are mixed. Read where they stand today — don't let a roadmapped
sovereign option dress a Leaking deal as Hedged.

## No Double-Counting — Boundaries with Neighboring Tests

Score each signal once, in its own row.

| Neighboring test | It tests | This lens adds | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LLM Ingestion Test** | *Outside-in*: could a lab model replicate 80% of the build from **public** data? | *Inside-out*: what does the lab learn from **this company's own traffic, tier, and terms**? A company can pass LLM Ingestion (unique data nobody has) and still LEAK (it ships that unique data through the API daily). | LLM Ingestion scores public replicability only; Alpha Sovereignty scores leakage of the non-public edge. |
| **Goliath Test** | Incumbent *capability + intent*: could a Goliath kill it with a feature in 12–18 months? | Whether the startup is **training its own Goliath** — supplying the demand signal that shortens those 12–18 months (the Cursor mechanism). | Goliath result stands on its own; alpha-exposure evidence may inform the Goliath timeframe note but is flagged once, here. |
| **AI Wrapper Risk** | Replicability of the *build* (API orchestration vs proprietary layer). | Direction of alpha *flow* and dependency posture. A defensible non-wrapper can still leak. | Wrapper scores the build; this scores the flow. |
| **Agent-Era Exposure** | Demand-side: does the **problem survive** agents? | Supply-chain: given the problem survives, **who captures the alpha**? A deal can be Riding and Leaking at once. | Agent-Era never scores dependency/leakage; this lens never scores problem-dissolution. |

## Live Pitch Question Templates (when LEAKS or MIXED on ADVANCE)

Tailor to the deal — these are shapes, not scripts:

- "Walk us through what happens to [the specific alpha] when the product runs —
  what transits the model provider, on what tier, under what terms have you
  actually verified?"
- "If [primary provider] shipped a competing native product tomorrow — the Cursor
  scenario — how long until you're running elsewhere at comparable quality, and
  what evidence backs that?"
- "Which of your target buyers already require data to stay inside their boundary,
  and what does saying yes cost you?"

## The Resonating Takeaway

*"The frontier labs don't steal your data — the terms forbid it, and they don't
need it. What they take is legal, and no contract can stop it: they watch your
traffic prove your market, they pay wholesale for the intelligence you buy retail,
and once your category is demonstrated they ship it themselves at a price you
cannot match. They take the business, not the bytes. In the AI era, the
intelligence is rented. The alpha is what you own. Don't fund companies that can't
tell the difference."*
